About the artist

A practice built on curiosity.

Illustration, animation, design, and code: different ways into an idea, and different ways to make it felt.

01 / Introduction

Hello, I’m Duy.

I’m Duy Le, an illustrator and digital artist working across illustration, animation, design, and creative coding. I enjoy creating illustrations inspired by Japanese-style animation and exploring different mediums to find new ways into an idea.

02 / Artist statement

Artist Statement

I am a digital illustrator and animator whose work is rooted in anime and fan culture. I grew up admiring artists who built entire worlds through illustration and animation, and that admiration became the standard I hold myself to. My work focuses on translating feeling into visual form. whether through character-driven animation, interactive code, or experimental video. I treat software as part of my creative voice, not just a tool.

In my Digital Animation Project, created in After Effects for Art 74, I built a short animation that simulates the experience of playing a game. The final piece features a clean, readable UI, level progression, sound effects, and a character with flapping wings, all designed to feel like a game trailer. The central challenge was learning the curves of animation. As someone trained in drawing, I had to retrain my instincts. Frames fell out of order. Resolutions broke down because I worked across multiple programs. Animating the wings to feel organic was time-consuming. These failures forced me to refine my workflow and persist until the piece functioned as a whole.

In my Creative Coding project for Art 74, I used p5.js to build an interactive character with movement. The final piece is a simple figure that responds on screen through code, with a clean visual layout and no unnecessary elements. The challenge was coding itself. I had no prior experience, so I researched how functions interacted and applied them to my own code. I crashed the program repeatedly and fixed it each time. The intention was to write functional code where every line served a purpose.

In Technology Paradoxes, created in Premiere Pro for Art 75, I made a short experimental video about how technology has controlled humans as a whole. The final piece uses only cuts, pacing, and unsettling visual keys, no words, to communicate the message. Learning Premiere Pro for the first time to materialize my idea was difficult, but I sketched the shots I felt could be impactful and built the sequence around them.

Across my earlier coursework in Art 74, I also used Photoshop and Illustrator to produce raster and vector images for web and print, and I learned HTML5 and CSS to understand how digital art lives online. That foundation shaped how I think about software culture, remix, and the social graph.

What connects these projects is my belief that technique should serve feeling. I do not make work to show off software. I make work to understand myself and to invite the viewer to feel something. Looking back at my journey at Chabot College and SJSU, I have learned that resilience and discipline matter more than perfect results. I want to combine multiple mediums in my personal art and continue growing as an artist. If my work can make people feel, even a little, then I am satisfied.
