About the artist

A practice built on curiosity.

01 / Introduction

Hello, I’m Duy.

I’m Duy Le, an illustrator and digital artist working across illustration, animation, design, and creative coding. I enjoy creating illustrations inspired by Japanese-style animation and exploring different mediums to find new ways into an idea.

02 / Artist statement

Artist Statement

My artistic identity is rooted in anime and fan culture. I grew up admiring artists who built entire worlds through illustration and animation, and that admiration became a standard I hold for myself. As a Digital Media Art student at San José State University, I work primarily in digital drawing, illustration, and animation. My toolset includes Procreate, Adobe Premiere Pro, Illustrator, Photoshop, Lightroom, and After Effects for coursework, as well as Blender and Clip Studio Paint for personal projects. I treat software as more than a functional tool—it is part of my creative voice, and I use it deliberately to translate ideas into visual form.

Critical thinking and problem-solving define how I approach my work. In my Digital Animation Project, I used After Effects to simulate the experience of playing a game, complete with levels, sound effects, and a clean UI designed to feel like a game trailer. The central challenge was learning the curves of animation. As someone trained in drawing, I had to retrain my instincts. I learned to be consistent, to observe carefully, and to assess the entire composition rather than a single frame. My iterative process was difficult. Frames fell out of order, and resolutions broke down because I worked across multiple programs. Animating the flapping wings to feel organic was especially time-consuming. These failures forced me to refine my workflow, check my work systematically, and persist until the piece functioned as a whole.

In my Creative Coding project, I used p5.js to build a simple character with movement. The challenge was coding itself. I had no prior experience, so I researched how functions interacted and applied them to my own code. I explored ideas while simultaneously trying to visualize them. I crashed the program repeatedly and fixed it each time. The intention was to write clean, functional code with no unnecessary lines. Everything had to work and make sense the way it was supposed to.

In Technology Paradoxes, I used video techniques to express how technology has controlled humans as a whole. My challenge was communicating a message without words, relying only on cuts and editing. Learning Premiere Pro for the first time to materialize my idea was difficult, but my iterative process was clearer than I expected. I sketched the visual keys I felt could be impactful and unsettling, reflecting how technology like AI makes us feel.

Looking back at my journey at Chabot College and SJSU, I have learned to be more creative and to keep exploring myself even when I am wrong. The most important lessons I have learned are resilience and discipline. As an artist in a field that always aims for perfect results, I remind myself to keep doing what I love, stay serious about my path, and enjoy the process. I want to translate everything I have learned into my personal art and combine multiple mediums.

When viewers experience my work, I hope it channels my feelings and makes them feel something. If my work can make people feel, even a little, then I am satisfied.
